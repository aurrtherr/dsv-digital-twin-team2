**Digital Twin Project Brief** **40.318 Supply Chain Digitalization and Design**
Digital Twin Project Brief 
1. Background Story 
We operate large-scale logistics warehouses that support global customers from industries such  as healthcare, high-tech, industrial manufacturing, and consumer products. 
A typical warehouse includes handling daily inbound receipts, inventory storage, order picking,  packing, and outbound shipments for multiple customers. Operations rely on accurate  coordination between inventory, storage capacity, and order fulfilment activities. 
Despite having a large amount of operational data available, supervisors and managers often  face a common challenge: 
"We have data everywhere, but we do not always have the information needed to make  decisions quickly." 
**Today, information is spread across multiple systems**. Operations managers may need to  consult separate dashboards to understand order status, inventory levels, automation  performance, labour deployment, equipment downtime, and warehouse capacity utilisation. 
As warehouse operations become increasingly automated and dynamic, there is a growing need  for a Digital Twin that provides a single source of truth and supports real-time operational  decision making. 
2. Problem Statement 
You have been engaged as a Digital Innovation Team tasked with designing the next generation  Digital Twin for one of our warehouses. 
**Your mission is not merely to create a dashboard.** 
Instead, your goal is to answer a more important question: 
"How can a Digital Twin help warehouse managers understand what is happening right  now, why it is happening, and what actions they should take next?" 
Using the provided datasets, students are expected to explore how warehouse data can be  transformed into meaningful operational visibility, predictive insights, and decision-support  capabilities.

Digital Twin Project Brief 
4. What Is a Digital Twin? 
For the purpose of this project, a Digital Twin is: 
A live digital representation of a warehouse that combines operational data, assets,  inventory, people, and processes into a single interactive environment. 
A Digital Twin should enable users to: 
• Observe the current state of operations 
• Identify bottlenecks and abnormalities 
• Understand trends over time 
• Simulate future scenarios 
• Receive data-driven recommendations 
**Think beyond traditional reporting dashboards.** 
A successful Digital Twin should function as a **virtual command centre** for warehouse  operations and guide managers in decision making. 
5. What Would a Good Digital Twin Do? 
A great Digital Twin should help users understand **why it is happening, what will happen next,  and what they should do about it.** 
Think of the Digital Twin as moving through four levels of maturity:

| Maturity Description Outcome |  |  |
|---|---|---|
| **Level 1: Visibility** | Shows what is happening now. Provide a real-time view of  warehouse operations. | Managers gain visibility without  opening multiple systems. |
| **Level 2: Diagnosis** | Explains why something is  happening. Help users identify  root causes. | Managers spend less time  investigating problems. |
| **Level 3: Prediction** | Forecasts future operational  conditions. Anticipate issues  before they occur. | Managers move from reactive to  proactive decision-making. |
| **Level 4:**<br>**Recommendation** | Suggests actions. Provide  actionable recommendations. | The Digital Twin becomes a  decision-support system rather  than a dashboard. |
